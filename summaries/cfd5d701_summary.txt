Kerala, India, experienced severe monsoon rains and floods beginning August 8, 2018, described as the worst since 1924. These floods followed a period of excessive rainfall since the start of the monsoon season in June, exceeding normal levels by over 40%, leading to dams overflowing and rivers breaching their banks.

The disaster has caused at least 230 deaths with over 360 fatalities since June, and the death toll is expected to rise as waters recede. Approximately 725,000 people have been displaced and are sheltering in 5,645 relief camps across the state. Infrastructure damage includes a closed Kochi airport (flooded runways since August 15), and disrupted rail services, though some train lines have resumed with speed regulations.

Rescue operations have reportedly saved around 22,000 people, and 90% of the rescues are complete. Relief efforts include the provision of food, water, medicines, and the restoration of essential services like power, fuel, telecom, and transport. The National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC) is prioritizing these needs. Health officials are addressing potential outbreaks of waterborne and airborne diseases in the relief camps, with the WHO sending a 13-member team to assist.

Currently, the rains have subsided, and no red alert is in effect for Kerala's districts. The focus is shifting to cleanup, relief, rehabilitation, and preventing disease outbreaks. The INS Garuda naval base in Kochi is open for commercial flights. While rescue efforts have diminished, there is a focus on recovery as donations pour in.
