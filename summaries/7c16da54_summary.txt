Between August 8 and 16, Kerala experienced its worst floods in nearly a century. In the first 20 days of August, the state received 771 mm of rainfall, 179% above the norm. This extreme rainfall forced the opening of the sluice gates of 78 out of 80 dams, including those at Mullaperiyar and Idukki in the Western Ghats. The sudden release of water caused severe flooding in Ernakulam, Idukki, Kottayam, Pathanamthitta, Alappuzha, and Thrissur districts.

The disaster resulted in over 400 fatalities and displaced over 720,000 people, a number comparable to the population of Puducherry. The floods impacted a wide range of areas, from highlands and cities to lower plains, which is a first for the state. Over 40,000 hectares of farmland were submerged, and 26,000 houses were severely damaged. The floods also inundated one of the state's three international airports, located in Kochi, causing its temporary closure.
