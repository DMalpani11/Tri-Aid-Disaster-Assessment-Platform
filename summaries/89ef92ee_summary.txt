In August 2018, Kerala, India, experienced severe floods exacerbated by mismanagement of dams. Madhavan Nair Rajeevan, Secretary of the Ministry of Earth Sciences, acknowledged the lack of scientific decision support systems for reservoir operations and flood management across India. He stated that this deficiency hampered informed decisions about when and how to release water from dams. Kerala also lacked an effective flood warning system.

Experts corroborated that the dam management worsened the floods. Despite warnings of heavy rainfall from the Indian Meteorological Department (IMD), the state failed to release water from nearly full dams in July before the heaviest rains arrived. An IMD official stated that the state should have taken the decision to release water from the dams that were almost full when the rains had subsided in July. A civil engineering professor estimated that flood damage could have been reduced by 20-40% if water had been released gradually over two weeks. Instead, water was released only when dams reached dangerous levels, leading to a surge that intensified the flooding. The news bulletin highlights the urgent need for decision support systems at dams to address climate change impacts.
