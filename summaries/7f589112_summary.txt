Kerala State, India, is experiencing severe flooding, reportedly the worst since 1924. This event occurred around August 16, 2018, and is expected to worsen due to continued rainfall predicted by the Indian Meteorological Service until August 19. All 14 districts are under red alert, with 35 dams opened, causing river levels to exceed alert thresholds across the state.

The floods have had a significant impact, with the death toll reported at 73 (as of August 16), including 25 fatalities on August 15 alone. Approximately 20 million people are reportedly affected, with landslides occurring in various regions. Transportation, power, communication, and drinking water distribution have been severely disrupted. Kochi airport operations are suspended until August 18.

Response efforts are underway. Over 150,000 people have been rescued and are housed in 1,068 relief camps throughout the state. Despite these efforts, the situation is expected to deteriorate further with continued rainfall and rising water levels. The public transport system has collapsed in many parts of central Kerala.
