Kerala, a southern Indian state, experienced its worst floods, beginning at the end of May and intensifying with incessant downpours since August 8. The event is described as a "huge disaster," resulting in widespread devastation and displacement.

The disaster has caused significant loss of life, with the death toll reaching at least 370. Over 800,000 people have been displaced and moved to shelters, while the total number of affected individuals, including those living with relatives or friends, is estimated to be well over 1.5 million. Infrastructure damage is estimated at almost $3 billion.

Rescue operations are underway, involving thousands of personnel from the army, air force, navy, and the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF). Airdropping supplies and rescuing those marooned in isolated areas are the primary focuses. Notably, fishermen from across Kerala have played a crucial role, utilizing approximately 500 boats to navigate interior waterways and assist in rescue efforts.

As of Sunday, when the rains began to subside, 22,000 people were evacuated from their homes. However, a significant number of people are voluntarily remaining in their flooded homes, often living on upper floors. The overall situation remains dire, with millions affected by the flooding.
